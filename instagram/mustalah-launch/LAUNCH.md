Two carousels, 1080 x 1440, in this folder. Regenerate with `npm run dev` then
`npm run instagram:mustalah` (script: `scripts/instagram-mustalah-launch.mts`).

and pin the series carousel. Every later part follows the existing rule in
`instagram/README.md`: a card with a small, self-explanatory diagram. Never post a
date or a part number for a lesson that is not written yet.

Topics named after Part 1 on the road-ahead slide (series slide 5) come from the
class notes headings, not from finished posts. Re-check that slide when Part 2 is
written, and change it if the order changes.

## Caption: series carousel

How do we know a hadith is authentic?

Abdullah bin al-Mubarak said: "The Isnaad (chain of narration) is part of the Deen.
Were it not for the Isnaad, anyone could say whatever they wanted." (Sahih Muslim,
Introduction 32)

Behind the chain lies a whole science, built to tell the authentic from the weak. It
is called Mustalah al-Hadith, and we are starting it from the very beginning, one
step at a time, in a new course: Mustalah Hadith Made Easy.

We follow Min Atyab al-Minah fi Ilm al-Mustalah by Shaykh Abdul Muhsin Al-Abbad and
Shaykh Abdul Kareem al-Murad. Beginner friendly. Free to read.

Part 1 is live: arriqaaq.com/courses/mustalah-hadith

## Caption: Part 1 carousel

Part 1 of Mustalah Hadith Made Easy is out: a brief history.

Where did the science of hadith terminology come from? From the sahifas written in
the time of the Sahabah, to the first works, to Ibn al-Salah and the books that grew
around him.

Inside the lesson: the story in five steps, a narrator you can take out of the chain,
a game to put the books in order, questions after every section, every scholar named
with a card, and a quiz of eight questions with each answer explained.

About 21 minutes to read. The next post begins on the science itself.

arriqaaq.com/mustalah-hadith-made-easy-part-1-brief-history
